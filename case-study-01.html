Case Study 01Cell Battery Assembly Workflow Transformation

Author: Jonas Bonnett
Fictional setting: Meridian Cell Systems (fictional company) — Cell Battery Assembly Operations
Illustrative role: CAD Designer III & Lead Assembly Technician

FICTIONAL ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: This case study is written to demonstrate production-workflow transformation method. The company, department, and figures are fictionalized and do not describe work performed at any real employer. It is not a record of employment. All figures below are adjusted representative values based on real-world experience, not exact records.

Context

The cell battery assembly line needed to move from ad-hoc builds to a repeatable, high-throughput operation feeding the testing and validation scientist team. In this example, one owner carried the mechanical workflow end to end — from design through assembly, test readiness, and release. The chain ran from the Masterplan and tracking-sheet documentation through manufacturing to release: the documentation governed the build, and the build fed the release — one flow, one owner.

Challenge

  • Assembly sub-assemblies required redesign for robustness and manufacturability.
  • No standardized build process: throughput, quality, and traceability varied run to run.
  • PCB boards needed retrofit fixtures to integrate reliably with the fixture design.
  • Parts flow — shipping and receiving through final release — lacked a governed workflow.

What was done

  • Redesigned sub-assembly parts for robustness, making judgment calls in working sessions with the Senior Electrical Engineer, Senior Electronic Technician, and Senior Mechanical Engineering team.
  • Designed PCB board retrofit fixtures so boards integrated cleanly with the assembly.
  • Invented custom assembly tooling for the build process, targeting throughput bottlenecks identified on the line: multi-dimensional 3D wire guides, Top Plate assembly guides, and a Soldering stand — tested and incorporated into the optimized workflow.
  • Invented the 3D multidimensional wire guide — a 3D-printed fixture that holds the wire in its exact cut/form/bend path so every piece comes out consistent and solders cleanly into the PCB sideboards. Before the fixture, a technician made 2 pieces in 5 minutes by hand; with the fixture, 50 pieces in the same 5 minutes — a 25× throughput gain. The guide was written into the official prep work — 1,000 red and 1,000 black wires per 250-unit scope.
  • Assisted scale-up of automated build-line operations and troubleshooting — working the automated build line toward an 80-unit run with clean data to show potential investors, and scaling the process for high-throughput production.
  • Authored the Assembly Standard Operating Procedures, locking in the improved methods so gains survived personnel changes.
  • Created the Assembly Master Plan Architecture Framework — the governing workflow from parts receiving through assembly, QA, and release of approved units to the testing and validation team, with a roadmap for future developments.
  • Trained 8 Engineering Technicians in advanced techniques to full independent proficiency — reducing downtime and raising overall productivity.
  • Deployed a 4-phase station assembly system for the technicians, governed by the Masterplan architecture workflow sheet: Station 1 — Preparation (sub-assembly prep), Station 2 — Building, Station 3 — Installation (defect and alignment checks), Station 4 — Wiring.
  • Conducted regular process-improvement meetings (bi-weekly cadence) plus weekly scope alignment, fostering a culture of continuous improvement and collaboration.

Results

  • 700+ holders built across three generations — leading the Mechanical and Electrical Engineering teams.
  • Production increased 40% via the custom assembly tooling and optimized workflow.
  • Sustained rate of 8–12 complete units per week with team collaboration, tracked on the Masterplan sheet (project scope: 250 complete units; 1,000 top sub-assemblies; weekly updated defect, rework, and WIP quantities).
  • Wire-guide throughput: 2 pieces per 5 minutes by hand → 50 pieces per 5 minutes with the fixture — a 25× throughput gain (3D multidimensional wire guide).
  • Scope quantities (Masterplan sheet): 250 complete units; 1,000 top sub-assemblies; 250 rear sub-assemblies; 250 side panels; 1,000 red + 1,000 black prep wires; bottom plates.
  • Validation tracked per unit: unit IDs logged against board revisions with pass/flag/out-of-service disposition — a visible run shows mostly Passed, two flagged for further investigation, one out of service.
  • Automation scale-up: Automated build line operated and troubleshot toward an 80-unit investor-showcase build with clean data, scaling for high throughput.

Why it matters

This is what operational engineering leadership looks like: see the whole system, redesign the weak points, invent what doesn’t exist, document it so it sticks, and run the cadence that keeps it improving. The Masterplan framework is now a commercial product (JB Manufacturing Problem-Solving Playbook) because the method transfers to any production line.
