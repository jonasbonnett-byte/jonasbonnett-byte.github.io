Case Study 012Ah Pouch Cell Holder Workflow Transformation

Author: Jonas Bonnett
Fictional setting: Meridian Cell Systems (fictional company) — Pouch Cell Holder Operations
Illustrative role: CAD Designer III & Lead Pouch Cell Holder Assembler

FICTIONAL ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: This case study is written to demonstrate production-workflow transformation method. The company, department, and figures are fictionalized and do not describe work performed at any real employer. It is not a record of employment. All figures below are adjusted representative values based on real-world experience, not exact records.

Context

The 2Ah pouch cell holder line needed to move from ad-hoc builds to a repeatable, high-throughput operation feeding the testing and validation scientist team. In this example, one owner carried the mechanical workflow end to end — from holder design through assembly, test readiness, and release. The chain ran from the Masterplan and tracking-sheet documentation through manufacturing to release: the documentation governed the build, and the build fed the release — one flow, one owner.

Challenge

  • Pouch cell holder sub-assemblies required redesign for robustness and manufacturability.
  • No standardized build process: throughput, quality, and traceability varied run to run.
  • PCB boards needed retrofit fixtures to integrate reliably with the holder design.
  • Parts flow — shipping and receiving through final release — lacked a governed workflow.

What was done

  • Redesigned pouch cell holder sub-assembly parts for robustness, making judgment calls in working sessions with the Senior Electrical Engineer, Senior Electronic Technician, and Senior Mechanical Engineering team.
  • Designed PCB board retrofit fixtures so boards integrated cleanly with the holder assembly.
  • Invented custom assembly tooling for the holder build process, targeting throughput bottlenecks identified on the line: multi-dimensional 3D wire guides, Top Plate assembly guides, and a Holder Soldering stand — tested and incorporated into the optimized workflow.
  • Invented the 3D multidimensional wire guide — a 3D-printed fixture that holds the wire in its exact cut/form/bend path so every piece comes out consistent and solders cleanly into the PCB sideboards. Before the fixture, a technician made 2 pieces in 5 minutes by hand; with the fixture, 50 pieces in the same 5 minutes — a 25× throughput gain. The guide was written into the official prep work (Step 3: pre-cut wires to length, utilize 3D printed wire guide for routing and tin wires) — 1,000 red and 1,000 black wires per 250-holder scope.
  • Assisted scale-up of Pouch Build Automation machine operations and troubleshooting — working the automated build line toward an 80-cell run with clean data to show potential investors, and scaling the process to 5Ah pouch cells for high-throughput production.
  • Authored the Pouch Cell Holder Standard Operating Procedures, locking in the improved methods so gains survived personnel changes.
  • Created the Pouch Cell Holder Master Plan Architecture Framework — the governing workflow from parts receiving through assembly, QA, and release of approved units to the testing and validation team, with a roadmap for future developments.
  • Trained 8 Engineering Technicians in advanced techniques to full independent proficiency — reducing downtime and raising overall productivity.
  • Deployed a 4-phase station assembly system for the technicians, governed by the Masterplan architecture workflow sheet: Station 1 — Preparation (sub-assembly prep), Station 2 — Building (top PCB on compression plate, temp probe sub-assembly), Station 3 — Installation (side/rear PCBs, defect and alignment checks), Station 4 — Wiring (channel and thermistor wire soldering, UID tags, pins).
  • Conducted regular process-improvement meetings (bi-weekly cadence) plus weekly scope alignment, fostering a culture of continuous improvement and collaboration.

Results

  • 700+ pouch cell holders built, Gen 1 through Gen 3, for 12 towers — plus 250 holders for 2A battery testing — leading the Mechanical and Electrical Engineering teams.
  • Production increased 40% via the custom assembly tooling and optimized workflow.
  • Sustained rate of 8–12 complete holders per week with team collaboration, tracked on the Masterplan sheet (project scope: 250 complete holders; 1,000 top PCB sub-assemblies; weekly updated defect, rework, and WIP quantities).
  • Wire-guide throughput: 2 pieces per 5 minutes by hand → 50 pieces per 5 minutes with the fixture — a 25× throughput gain (3D multidimensional wire guide).
  • Scope quantities (Masterplan sheet): 250 complete holders; 1,000 top PCB sub-assemblies; 250 rear PCB sub-assemblies; 250 side PCBs; 1,000 red + 1,000 black prep wires; bottom plates with orange silicone.
  • Validation tracked per holder: holder IDs logged against top/side/back board revisions with pass/flag/out-of-service disposition — a visible run shows mostly Passed, two flagged for further investigation, one out of service.
  • Automation scale-up: Pouch Build Automation line operated and troubleshot toward an 80-cell investor-showcase build with clean data, scaling to 5Ah cells for high throughput.

Why it matters

This is what operational engineering leadership looks like: see the whole system, redesign the weak points, invent what doesn’t exist, document it so it sticks, and run the cadence that keeps it improving. The Masterplan framework is now a commercial product (JB Manufacturing Problem-Solving Playbook) because the method transfers to any production line.
