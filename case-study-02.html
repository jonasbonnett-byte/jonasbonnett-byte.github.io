Case Study 02Cross-Functional Technical Leadership — Lean Six Sigma Deployment

Author: Jonas Bonnett
Fictional setting: Meridian Cell Systems (fictional company) — Pouch Cell Manufacturing & Lab Operations
Illustrative role: Liaison, CAD Designer III & Lead Pouch Cell Holder Assembler

FICTIONAL ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE: This case study is written to demonstrate cross-functional technical leadership method. The company, department, and figures are fictionalized and do not describe work performed at any real employer. It is not a record of employment. All figures below are adjusted representative values based on real-world experience, not exact records.

Context

Deploying Lean Six Sigma thinking across pouch cell manufacturing and lab operations required more than process tools — it required alignment from the top of the engineering organization down to the technicians executing the work. In this example, one person operated across every layer of that stack.

Challenge

  • Lean Six Sigma methods needed adoption beyond a single team — across manufacturing and lab operations.
  • Engineering tracking systems (2Ah pouch cell holder sheets) were difficult to search and use, hiding the data decisions depended on.
  • Technical decisions spanned electrical, electronic, and mechanical domains with no single integrator.

What was done

  • Met with Directors and the President of Engineering to drive Lean Six Sigma thinking into pouch cell manufacturing and lab operations — presenting the case, aligning priorities, and securing leadership backing.
  • Made cross-functional judgment calls in working sessions with the Senior Electrical Engineer, Senior Electronic Technician, and Senior Mechanical Engineering team — redesigning sub-assemblies and fixtures with all disciplines at the table.
  • Partnered with a Senior Data Scientist to deploy a findability/organization strategy for the 2Ah pouch cell holder tracking sheets across the engineering department, making operational data accessible to the people making decisions.
  • Ran the operating cadence: bi-weekly process-improvement meetings and weekly scope alignment for the pouch cell holder team.
  • Pitched a logo t-shirt design to the CEO (Creation-of-Adam meets electron motif) — approved, produced, and worn. Creative leadership with executive visibility.
  • Introduced Lean Six Sigma methodologies to the operation — identifying and eliminating inefficiencies, producing cost savings and improved workflow.
  • Trained mechanical and electrical engineering technicians to independent proficiency, cutting downtime and lifting team productivity.

Results

  • Led the Mechanical and Electrical Engineering teams through 700+ holder builds (Gen 1→Gen 3, 12 towers) plus 250 units for 2A battery testing, with increased validation passing rates.

Team-building and unification

Encouraged team-building and unification across ALL departments by organizing the company Chess Club, introducing Double’s Chess — two teams competing against each other to dominate the board. With HR and CEO approval, created and ran a Chess Tournament, a Cornhole Tournament, and Table Tennis Tournaments — giving individuals a stage to showcase their talents, motivating others to step their game up, boosting team morale, and letting players shine at their best.

Why it matters

Influence without authority is the core skill of advisory work. This case shows it in practice: aligning directors and a president behind a methodology, integrating three engineering disciplines around shared judgment calls, and improving the information systems the whole department ran on. It’s the closest thing to fractional executive work you can do inside employment — and the credential that opens the door to doing it for the market.
