Project · November 2026The Bay Rig — 10-Foot Inflatable Fishing Boat

Author: Jonas Bonnett
Context: Personal project — San Diego Bay fishing
Role: Solo — study, rigging, launch and operation
Cost: $1,210 total ($450 inflatable, $500 engine, $60 transom wheels, $200 miscellaneous)
Result: A portable bay fishing boat: one person, no trailer, no slip, licensed operator

The problem

Fishing San Diego Bay from shore means fishing the piers and the shoreline. A boat opens the rest of it — the channels, the flats, the structure. But a conventional boat brings a trailer, a tow vehicle, storage, a slip, and thousands of dollars before a line ever gets wet. The project asked: get on the bay to fish, without any of that.

How it was built — study first, then rig

  • Studied the law first. Took the boating license — the California Boater Card, required for every operator of a motorized vessel on California waterways since January 1, 2025 — and learned the rules and regulations before the rig was complete.
  • Studied motor setups for different purposes over six months, and chose a 4-stroke 6 HP: quiet, fuel-efficient, no oil mixing, about 20 kg — the right size for protected bay water.
  • Bought a 10-foot inflatable and a 6 HP four-stroke outboard (140 cc, air-cooled, tiller steering, 1.2-litre built-in tank).
  • Installed transom wheels for portable transport — one person rolls the rig from the car to the water, no trailer, no second pair of hands.

The result

One person can take it from the car to San Diego Bay and fish, with no trailer, no slip, and no big boat. The operator is licensed, the rig is legal, and the motor is the right size for the water it runs. The transom wheels are the detail that makes the whole thing work — without them, a 10-foot inflatable with a 20-kg motor is a two-person carry; with them, it is a one-person roll.

What this is not

This is a bay boat, not an ocean boat — ten feet and 6 HP stays inside the bay. The full DIY paper — spec sheet, launch sequence, rules checklist, and open questions — is linked below.

Skills this demonstrates

Requirements-led design (portable, one-person, affordable, legal), research and regulation study, powerplant selection by purpose, and rigging a small boat for transport, launch, and fishing.
